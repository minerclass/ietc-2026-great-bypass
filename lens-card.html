The lens: four questions for any AI use

Run it on one specific use, not on a tool in general. Start from the learning goal: what must the learner still practice or decide?

Where friction lives

HeadWorking it out for yourself.noetic · protect
RoomSaying it so others understand.rhetorical · protect

The base: the conditionsTime, access, technology, policy, the building. What makes the other three possible at all.infrastructural · reduce the barriers here

Remove barriers in any layer. Preserve the thinking, dialogue, and responsibility required by the learning goal.

Four questions

  1. 01What does this make easier?Be specific. Saves whom what, at which step?
  2. 02Which layer is it easing?The base, or Head, Room, or World? If you cannot say, you cannot govern it.
  3. 03What is the student no longer doing, and was that the standard?If the removed step was the objective, the tool did not help with the objective. It replaced it.
  4. 04What signal would tell us it went wrong, and who is watching it?Ordinary completion data may not show a learning gap. Name the evidence and the person who will inspect it.

Your two sentences

In this use, must still . will watch for signs the support is replacing the work that matters.

For it to last, (grades, pacing, device settings, professional learning, or policy) has to support it, and owns that.

Example, middle school reading. In AI-summarized readings, 7th graders must still state the author's central claim in their own words. The ELA coach will review monthly student-authored exit tickets, with needed accessibility supports, for signs the tool is doing the target thinking. For it to last, the ELA pacing guide has to leave time for the exit tickets, and the curriculum director owns that.

What the lens does not do. It looks for displaced learning. It does not clear a use for privacy, accuracy, bias, access, or academic integrity. Those checks still apply, including when a tool eases only the base.
