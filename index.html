The Great Bypass

Pedagogical friction as response to tertiary algorithmicity in the age of algorithms and AI.

Micah J. Miner, CETL, Ed.S. · Director of Innovation & Technology, Beach Park CCSD 3 · Ed.D. Candidate, National Louis University · Author, AI Goes to School

Maya's essay:YouTubeGoogle Drive

Before we start

Two questions.

Talk to the people at your table. I want honest answers, not impressive ones.

Question one

Name one thing that got easier in your classroom, school, or district this year because of AI.

Question two

Now name one thing your students got better at because of AI.

The gap

Those are not the same question.

"Got easier"

  • Mostly about adults
  • Measured in workload
  • Visible this year
  • Easy to point to

"Got better"

  • About students
  • Measured in capability
  • Visible in independent work
  • Harder to infer from output alone

It is easy to answer the first and believe we have answered the second. The question underneath both is the one this session is about: what should stay difficult?

Why the metric fails

Two students. Forty-five minutes each.

Both students 45 min · complete · on task Your data cannot tell them apart.

The better question

Not how long the screen was on. What the screen made easy.

This is the Great Bypass. The screen looks the same whether the student did the work or the work went around the student.

What learning science says

Some difficulty is how learning happens.

01

Desirable difficulties

Spacing, retrieval, and generating an answer slow performance in the moment and strengthen what lasts (Bjork & Bjork, 2011).

02

Productive failure

Struggling with a problem before instruction can deepen understanding afterward (Kapur, 2008).

03

Performance is not learning

A correct product can arrive without the understanding it was supposed to show (Kapur, 2016).

04

The Great Bypass

Generative AI makes each of these difficulties optional by default. The product still appears.

The question is not whether students use AI. It is which difficulty the use removes, and whether that difficulty was the learning.

Researchers call this unproductive success

Green dashboard, unfinished learning

Illustrative pattern, not measured student data.

Tertiary algorithmicity · who makes the words

Why this moment is different.

For most of history, people made the words and people chose which ones reached us. Social media handed the choosing to algorithms. Generative AI hands over the making. I call that environment tertiary algorithmicity: algorithmic systems both curate and generate the symbolic content students learn inside, making human authorship optional at scale.

The reframe

Friction is not one thing.

I call the response pedagogical friction: the cognitive, rhetorical, and existential resistance durable learning requires. The research names: Head is noetic, Room is rhetorical, World is existential, and the base is infrastructural friction.

Remove barriers in any layer. Preserve the thinking, dialogue, and responsibility required by the learning goal.

An Illinois high school junior, writing in the September 29 ISBE Weekly: “To learn is to endure a necessary friction.”
Reduce this layer

The base: the conditions

The conditions that make learning possible at all. Time, access, technology, policy, the building itself.

A student who cannot reach an archive. A family who cannot read the notice. A teacher who loses an evening to scheduling. These are barriers to learning, not the learning itself. The research name is exclusionary friction. Removing them expands who gets to do the work.

The three you protect

Head, Room, and World.

Click each one

Head · the thinking work

Forming an idea, holding two things that contradict each other, staying in not-knowing long enough to get somewhere.

When a tool does this: the answer still appears. The thinking moved from the student to the system.

Room · the work of being understood

Finding the words, ordering them, imagining a reader who does not already agree with you.

When a tool does this: writing is not the transcription of a finished thought. Writing is how the thought gets finished. Fluent prose a student did not build skips the part where understanding forms.

World · the work of becoming

Who the student turns into by staying with something difficult until it is done.

When a tool does this: a student who never finishes something difficult may never learn that they can. That is not information. It is formation.

The part that is nobody's fault

The instructional judgment stays with you.

Tools embody decisions about what to make easier. Schools and districts still have to judge whether those decisions serve the learning goal and the learner's needs.

Which means the judgment call can only live with the adults. No procurement process, no setting, and no vendor assurance moves that responsibility off your desk.

The base decides what survives

Infrastructure is where friction lives or dies.

One teacher can redesign a task. Grades, pacing, device settings, professional learning, and policy decide whether the redesign survives the next grading period.

Research in progress, not findings. My doctoral study at National Louis University (IRB exempt, protocol ER01884) is collecting data through February 2027. One of its research questions:

“How do educators and school-system leaders make sense of and respond to the policy, professional-learning, assessment, access, and governance conditions surrounding generative AI?”

  • →Classroom-facing educatorsHow AI changes the work students do and how teachers judge it.
  • →Building-level administratorsSchool guidance, professional learning, and assessment expectations.
  • →District or system-level leadersPolicy, governance, and the decisions that enable or constrain classroom practice.

Where we started

The schools that will be ready are not the ones that adopted fastest.

They are the ones that decided, on purpose, what to keep difficult.
